Sewer Scope Video Diagnostics: Avoiding $15,000 Mainline Pipe Collapses

By John R. Davis, CMI® • Updated September 28, 2026 • 5 Min Read

When you fall in love with a home, it's easy to admire the brand-new quartz countertops and newly refinished hardwood floors. But cosmetic upgrades frequently mask severe, five-figure defects lying dormant in the attic, crawlspace, or electrical panel.

As licensed Master Inspectors who have evaluated more than 14,000 properties, we have seen hundreds of buyers almost close on homes with structural hazards that sellers hoped would go unnoticed. Below are the top red flags you must look out for during your escrow inspection contingency.

1. Compromised Chimney Flashing & Shingle Uplift

The intersection where a brick chimney penetrates asphalt shingle roofing is the #1 source of hidden roof leaks. If step flashing is rusted or sealed with temporary tar mastic, water slowly rots the roof deck sheathing. In severe cases, attic rafters deteriorate before any water stains show through the interior ceiling.

Inspector Tip: Always verify that your inspector either walked the roof or deployed an aerial 4K drone to check the mortar crown and flashing at close proximity.

2. Double-Tapped Circuit Breakers & Overamped Panels

A "double tap" occurs when an amateur DIYer or unlicensed handyman connects two separate circuit wires into a single circuit breaker slot. Unless the breaker is specifically engineered for two conductors (like select Square D QO models), this creates loose connections, arcing, and severe house fire hazards.

3. Foundation Slope & Lateral Wall Bowing

Not all foundation cracks are created equal. Minor vertical hairline cracks (< 1/16") are typically harmless shrinkage from initial concrete curing. However, horizontal stair-step cracks in block foundations or bowing greater than 1 inch indicate hydrostatic soil pressure that could require $15,000 to $40,000 in carbon fiber straps or steel piers.

4. Missing Water Heater TPR Discharge Tubes

Water heaters can build immense steam pressure if the thermostat fails. Every unit has a Temperature & Pressure Relief (TPR) valve. If this valve lacks an unthreaded copper or CPVC discharge pipe pointing within 6 inches of the floor, high-pressure boiling water can discharge directly into anyone standing nearby.

How to Use These Findings in Negotiation

When our digital report identifies any of these red flags, do not panic. Use the built-in VeriHome Repair Request Addendum tool. Select the defect, attach our photo proof, and have your agent submit a request for an escrow credit or licensed contractor repair before your contingency period closes.
